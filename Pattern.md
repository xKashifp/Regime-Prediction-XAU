# Chart pattern reference: long-term bullish vs long-term bearish

## Long-term bullish pattern

A sustained uptrend price structure, characterized by:

- **Higher highs (HH)**: each new swing peak is priced above the previous swing peak.
- **Higher lows (HL)**: each new swing trough is priced above the previous swing trough (pullbacks don't fall as far as the last one).
- **Ascending support line**: a trendline drawn under the higher lows slopes upward.
- **Moving averages**: shorter-term moving average stays above longer-term moving average, both sloping upward.
- **Overall shape**: a rising staircase — price zigzags but each zig and zag lands higher than the last.

## Long-term bearish pattern

A sustained downtrend price structure, characterized by:

- **Lower highs (LH)**: each new swing peak is priced below the previous swing peak (rallies fail earlier each time).
- **Lower lows (LL)**: each new swing trough is priced below the previous swing trough.
- **Descending resistance line**: a trendline drawn over the lower highs slopes downward.
- **Moving averages**: shorter-term moving average stays below longer-term moving average, both sloping downward.
- **Overall shape**: a falling staircase — price zigzags but each zig and zag lands lower than the last.

## Quick distinction from "ranging"

Both patterns above are directional/trending. A ranging (sideways) market instead shows swing highs and swing lows both staying within a roughly flat, repeating band — no consistent HH/HL or LH/LL sequence.
